Move a song to another album

The one question: which album does this song belong to? The control: the song's ⋯ gets one row, Move ›. Tapping it turns the same popover into the list of your albums. Tap an album and the song moves. You stay on the song, and the crumb (desktop) or the toast (phone) names the new album. Nothing is lost: the pick, keeps, versions, takes, the song's cover and its share link all move with the song. Rows with a dashed violet border are new. Everything else is today's song page, as song-page.html draws it.

Desktop 1440 the song page header; the song menu opens under its ⋯

Phone 375 the same menu and the same panel, 44 px rows, the right edge pinned 8 px inside the screen

States the panel is the same at both widths; each cut-out is the popover alone

What moves with the song read from today's route, PUT /songs/{id}/album

Comes along, unchanged

  • Versions and takes, including a take still rendering: it finishes inside the moved song.
  • ★ Pick and ♥ keeps. A pick belongs to the song (one per song), not to an album, so the move cannot clash with anything on the new album.
  • The song's own cover, stored under the song. A song without its own cover shows the new album's cover.
  • The song's share link and its takes' share links: they point at a share id, not at the album.
  • Playlists: they hold takes, so the song stays in every playlist.

Changes

  • The address changes from /album/homeoffice/storytime to /album/neon-harbour/storytime. If the new album already holds a “storytime”, even a deleted one, the address becomes storytime-2. The old address stops working.
  • Shared album pages: the song leaves Homeoffice's public page and appears on Neon Harbour's, with its pick, or its newest playable take when it has no pick (F3).
  • Its place in the album (F4). Song counts: Homeoffice one fewer, Neon Harbour one more.
  • Does not change, but worth knowing: takes rendered before the move keep the old album's name and track number in their MP3 tags. A download still says “Homeoffice”. That is a follow-up, not part of this item.

Decisions blessed 05.11.2026

F1 · Where the control sits

Only in the song menu, as one row Move › between Add to playlist and Delete song (D1, P1). Every affordance exists once; the song menu already exists at both widths.

F2 · What the picker lists

Your albums as the Library lists them, not archived, sorted the same way; the current album pinned on top with ✓ and not tappable, a filter, and rows that look like rail rows: cover or initials, title, song count, share mark (D2). Seeing where the song is now answers half the question, and an archived album would hide the moved song from the Library.

F3 · Moving into a shared album publishes the song

Confirm first. A move into a shared album makes a song public that was not public before; it is rare, so the extra tap costs little.

F4 · Where the song lands in the new album

At the end, as the last track, the way a new song does (D3). Keeping the old number would tie with another track in an order nobody chose, and the app has no reordering to fix it.

F5 · Where you are after the move

Stay on the song, with no Undo (D3, P3): same tab, same draft, same playing take; the crumb (desktop) or a toast (phone) names the new album, and the new address replaces the old one in the history. The person was working on the song; moving back is the same menu with two taps, so Undo would be a second way to do one thing.

F6 · You have only one album

The picker says “No other album” (S3). Albums are made in one place, Library › + NEW; a second album-making surface would bring its own failure states.

F7 · An admin

A move stays within one person's albums. The picker lists only your own albums, and the route refuses another person's song or album, admins included. Moving a song to another person is a change of owner, not a move, and one wrong tap in a long list must not hand a song away.
