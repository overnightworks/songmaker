# Songmaker vision

## What it does

Songmaker turns a musician's ideas into finished albums. Making one more take
is easy; keeping track of the lyrics, style, settings and many results behind
it is hard. Songmaker is the one place where that work stays together. Today a
musician can:

- Gather songs into albums, and listen across all their albums and playlists.
- Rework lyrics, style and settings, and always see which take came from which
  choices, even after many rounds.
- Steer a take with a voice trained on their own recordings, or with a
  reference recording.
- Compare many takes of a song, choose the one that stands for the album, and
  mark any number of others as favourites to protect.
- Ask a co-writer for help, and use transcription and scoring as aids.
- Share an album, song, take or playlist for anyone to hear without signing in.
- Let their own agent do part of this work for them.

Where Songmaker is heading:

- A finished album leaves Songmaker as a release: in the musician's order, at
  one loudness, each file carrying its title, cover and lyrics.
- Every take can be made again from what Songmaker recorded about it, with the
  same inputs; when something it depended on has changed or is gone since,
  Songmaker names what.
- Songmaker learns from the takes a musician chooses and protects what works
  for them, and shows the takes that prove it; it proposes, the musician
  decides.
- The co-writer hears the take; when it judges one, it names the seconds it
  means, and the musician can play exactly those.

The musician stays the author and curator: every aid advises and none decides.
What went into a take and what the musician chose can always be traced. When
something is missing, unavailable or unproven, Songmaker says so. It never
shows a success that did not happen or swaps in a different take unannounced.

## Who uses it today

Musicians admitted to Songmaker, each with their own albums and their own
conversation with the co-writer, some also through their own agent; and agents
with an account of their own, asked in a call to write and share a song.

## Deliberate non-goals

- Scores never choose the album take. The musician does.
- Marking a favourite never makes it the album take. Choosing and protecting
  are separate acts, and neither replaces the other.
